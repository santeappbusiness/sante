# Jue’s Santé writing and social guide

This is a starting point, not a script you have to defend word for word. Read it once, then
change the sentences until they sound like something you would actually say to a friend.

## Santé in five simple points

1. **The problem:** Women do not have the same energy, comfort, mood or tolerance for noise and
   decisions every day, but most wellness plans stay fixed.
2. **The idea:** Santé keeps the movement goal and changes the route for today.
3. **How it works:** A woman checks in, Santé adapts the workout she already planned, and it
   explains why.
4. **Why it matters:** A smaller session or a rest day is not treated as failure.
5. **What makes the AI responsible:** Our own code decides the safety boundary and limits first.
   The AI is only allowed to work inside them.

If you remember only one line, use this:

> We built Santé for the days when the goal still matters, but the original route no longer
> fits.

### Keep these facts accurate

- Santé is a women’s wellness app built specifically for women.
- It adapts planned movement using energy, discomfort, mood and sensory load.
- It does not require a wearable.
- It does not diagnose or give medical advice.
- The safety check and limits happen outside the AI.
- Maya is a fictional demo user.
- Calm mode reduces interface and decision load. It is not a medical mode.
- Cycle history and Apple or Samsung health data are future ideas, not current features.
- Demonstrations are chosen by us from verified sources. Do not say every movement has a video
  until the remaining coverage is finished.

### Make it sound human

Do:

- Write in first-person plural: **we built, we noticed, we decided**.
- Include one thing that actually happened while building.
- Keep sentences short enough to say aloud.
- Say what the user sees instead of using technical words.
- Let the idea be emotional without making medical claims.

Avoid:

- “Santé leverages cutting-edge AI to revolutionize wellness.”
- “A seamless, holistic and empowering ecosystem.”
- Claiming we understand a woman’s body better than she does.
- Describing roadmap features as if they already work.
- Making every sentence begin with “We.”

For example:

> **Too formal:** Santé leverages artificial intelligence to dynamically optimize personalized
> exercise recommendations.

> **More like us:** She tells Santé what today feels like. The app reshapes the session she
> already planned and tells her exactly why it changed.

## Longer descriptions ready to adapt

### General Instagram or launch caption

> We built Santé because a changed day should not feel like a failed one.
>
> Most wellness apps give you a plan and expect your body, energy and surroundings to cooperate.
> Santé starts differently. A woman checks in with her energy, discomfort, mood and sensory
> load, and the app reshapes the movement she already planned. It may shorten the session,
> remove a movement, make the experience quieter, or offer rest. It also explains why, and she
> can always disagree.
>
> We keep the safety boundary outside the AI. Our code decides the limits first, the AI works
> inside them, and the result is checked again before it reaches the screen.
>
> No wearable. No guilt for changing the route. Just a plan that can meet today honestly.
>
> Santé is designed mobile-first. Try the demo on your phone: https://sante-chi.vercel.app/

### Longer LinkedIn description

> We have been building Santé, a women’s wellness app for something most fixed plans ignore:
> capacity changes from day to day.
>
> The user begins with a session she intended to do. A short check-in captures energy,
> discomfort, mood and sensory load. Santé then adapts that existing plan rather than generating
> a random workout. Every change is explained, the original remains available, and rest is
> treated as a valid plan rather than a broken streak.
>
> Our most important technical decision was deciding what the AI should not control. Red-flag
> safety checks and session limits are calculated in deterministic application code. The model
> can choose only from movements already allowed by those limits, and its answer is validated
> again before display. A deterministic fallback keeps the core experience working if the model
> cannot.
>
> Santé also includes Calm mode, intent-first workout discovery, live movement swaps, weekly
> planning, feedback memory and progress based on the user’s own history.
>
> The live prototype is best viewed on mobile: https://sante-chi.vercel.app/

### Best Use of AI post

> Our favourite AI decision in Santé was deciding what the AI is not allowed to decide.
>
> Safety runs first in ordinary code. The app calculates today’s limits. The model receives
> only movements that already fit those limits. Its proposed session is checked again, and a
> deterministic fallback takes over if needed.
>
> The AI helps Santé make a plan feel personal and explainable, but it cannot talk its way past
> the boundary. That restraint is the feature.

## A simple posting layout

For an Instagram carousel or stitched video, keep the story in this order:

1. **Hook:** “The plan was 30 minutes. Today could hold 15.”
2. **Problem:** Fixed wellness plans assume capacity is fixed too.
3. **Check-in:** Energy, discomfort, mood and sensory load.
4. **Transformation:** Show the real before and after inside Santé.
5. **Trust:** The safety boundary is outside the AI.
6. **Choice:** Start, adjust again, keep the original, or rest.
7. **Close:** “Keep the goal. Change the route.”

Use only a few hashtags so the writing still feels like a person wrote it:

`#WomensWellness #WellnessTech #ResponsibleAI #Hackathon`

## Final check before publishing

- Would I actually say this aloud?
- Did I describe the product in the first two sentences?
- Did I make a medical claim by accident?
- Did I call a future feature current?
- Did I use **we** for the team and names only for individual contributions?
- Did I include the mobile demo link?
- Did I add one detail that belongs specifically to our team?

If all seven answers are clear, it is ready.
